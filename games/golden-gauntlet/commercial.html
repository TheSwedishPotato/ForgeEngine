The Golden Gauntlet

A television commercial for Master Oldřich, armourer of Platnéřská street, Prague — broadcast in the autumn of 1403 and painted in the manner of the Netherlandish masters.

What is true in it

Platnéřská street in Prague's Old Town is named after the platnéři, the plate-armourers who worked there. Master Oldřich and his shop are invented.

Prices are given in kopy — a "threescore", the Bohemian unit of account of 60 Prague groschen. The figures in the commercial are for comedy, not from a price list.

The hounskull bascinet, with its pointed "pig-faced" visor, was the knightly helmet across Europe c. 1380–1420; the great helm was by then a tournament piece.

Passau blades were famous, and marked with the running wolf. Armour was tried against the crossbow before it was sold as proof.

1403: King Wenceslas IV was a prisoner in Vienna, and King Sigismund's Hungarian army, with Cuman horsemen, had campaigned around Kutná Hora that winter.
